## Which plan fits your workload

Match the plan to the constraint that will actually bite. For most small Linux jobs that constraint is RAM, not CPU.

- **Static site, single small WordPress install, WireGuard or OpenVPN endpoint, Discord or Telegram bot:** Sirius Special, 1 core / 1 GB / 20 GB, $2.81/mo. A bot or a VPN rarely needs more than 512 MB, and 1 GB leaves room for the OS and updates.
- **Cheapest thing that boots at all:** VPS Promo at $1.99/mo. The catch is the port. At 10 Mbps fair-share the ceiling is roughly 4 TB of traffic a month if you saturate it around the clock, which is fine for a low-traffic site or a personal SSH box and painful for anything serving media.
- **One site with a real database, or two or three small apps behind a reverse proxy:** Arcturus Special, 1 core / 2 GB / 30 GB at $4.01. If disk is the worry rather than RAM, Alpha Centauri gives 2 cores / 2 GB / 40 GB for $8.99.
- **Docker with several containers (Nextcloud, n8n, a proxy, their databases):** Vega Special, 2 cores / 4 GB / 80 GB at $7.79. Four containers plus two databases will not fit comfortably in 2 GB once the page cache fills.
- **Modded Minecraft, Rust or FiveM:** Yildun Special, 4 cores / 8 GB / 200 GB at $16.20. Modded Minecraft is memory-hungry and the JVM will take most of 8 GB.
- **Postgres, CI runners, a build box, or anything with a large working set:** Betelgeuse Special at $26.40 for 6 cores / 12 GB, or Altair Special at $47.40 for 8 cores / 24 GB / 600 GB.

JustHost's VPS pages describe the platform as KVM, which means your own kernel, so Docker and Podman install normally and you can set your own sysctl values.

## The limits that do not show up in the spec sheet

“Unmetered” on JustHost means there is no traffic counter. It does not mean a fast pipe. Every plan is sold with a port speed marked fair-share, and the page itself converts that speed into a monthly ceiling: 10 Mbps equals about 4 TB, 100 Mbps about 31 TB, 300 Mbps about 93 TB, 750 Mbps about 232 TB. Those figures assume constant saturation. A 10 Mbps port is the reason the $1.99 plan should not host anything with real traffic, and the 750 Mbps port is why the larger plans can serve media without metering.

Three more details are worth checking at the order step.

**Address type is priced.** JustHost lists IPv6-only VPS from $1.60/mo and IPv4 + IPv6 VPS from $2.60/mo, so the address set is part of the price. The Special and standard plan cards do not spell out which set they ship with. Confirm on the order summary whether your plan includes a dedicated IPv4.

**Some plans have a minimum term.** New Star, Double Star and Bright Star are marked as available only when you order 3 months or more, so you cannot test them month to month.

**The pages reviewed do not publish a refund window, a supported distribution list or a setup time.** That is not proof they are absent, only that they are not on the tariff pages. Ask about all three before paying, and get the refund answer before you prepay a year.

## FAQ

**How much RAM do I need for a Linux VPS?**
512 MB runs a VPN endpoint or a bot. 1 GB runs a small site with a database. 4 GB is the sensible floor for several Docker containers. 8 GB and up is for game servers, JVM workloads or anything holding a large dataset in memory.

**Is $1.99 the price I keep paying?**
It is the current monthly rate on the Promo plan. The 12-month rate for that plan is not displayed, and no renewal price is published for any plan on either page. Get the renewal figure in writing before you prepay.

**Can I run Docker on these plans?**
Yes. KVM virtualisation gives you your own kernel, so Docker, Podman and compose files install normally. Choose by NVMe size rather than core count, because image pulls and database I/O hit the disk first.

**Do I get a dedicated IPv4 address?**
JustHost prices IPv6-only VPS separately from IPv4 + IPv6 VPS, so the answer depends on the plan. Check the order summary; if it does not say IPv4, ask before paying.

**Can I get a refund if the server is slow?**
No refund window is published on the tariff pages reviewed. Ask support directly and keep the reply, especially if you are paying for 12 months up front.
